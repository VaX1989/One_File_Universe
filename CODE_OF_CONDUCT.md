# One File Universe Code of Conduct

## Our standard

OFU welcomes participation across software engineering, science, design, accessibility, research, documentation, testing, art, education, and community work. Technical disagreement is expected; hostility and exclusion are not.

Participants are expected to:

- discuss ideas and evidence rather than attack people;
- make space for newcomers and explain project-specific concepts when practical;
- give and receive review without humiliation, gatekeeping, or status games;
- acknowledge uncertainty, mistakes, prior art, and contributions accurately;
- avoid harassment, threats, discrimination, sexual harassment, stalking, doxxing, or unwanted publication of private information;
- avoid using employer, academic, maintainer, financial, or founder status to bypass normal technical review;
- respect scientific and licensing provenance even when doing so slows a desired feature;
- stop disruptive behavior when a maintainer asks for it to stop.

## Scope

This policy applies in repository issues, pull requests, reviews, project-controlled discussion spaces and events, and when someone publicly represents themselves as acting on behalf of OFU.

## Enforcement

Reports should be handled privately whenever they contain sensitive personal information. Enforcement contacts are listed in `MAINTAINERS.md`; while the project remains in bootstrap governance, the repository owner is the escalation point. Do not put sensitive conduct reports in public issues.

Maintainers may edit/remove content, warn participants, temporarily restrict participation, or permanently ban participation when behavior materially harms people or the project. Serious cases should be documented privately enough to support consistent enforcement while minimizing unnecessary disclosure.

People responsible for a report involving themselves must recuse from its adjudication when another qualified maintainer is available.

## Technical criticism is allowed

This Code of Conduct does not require agreement, politeness theater, or suppression of rigorous criticism. Statements such as “this evidence does not support the claim,” “this API violates the authority contract,” or “this proposal should be rejected” are legitimate when focused on the work and supported appropriately.
