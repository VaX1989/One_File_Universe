# One File Universe Code of Conduct

## Purpose

OFU brings together software engineering, science, graphics, simulation, accessibility, research, documentation, testing, art and education. Deep disagreement is expected in a project this ambitious. Personal hostility, exclusion and abuse are not required for rigorous work and are not accepted as a substitute for evidence.

## Expected conduct

Participants are expected to:

- discuss ideas, behavior, code and evidence rather than attack people;
- engage honestly and in good faith, including when strongly disagreeing;
- respect different expertise, backgrounds, communication styles and levels of project familiarity;
- give and receive review without humiliation, hazing, gatekeeping or status games;
- make reasonable space for newcomers to learn project-specific concepts;
- acknowledge uncertainty, mistakes, prior art, sources and contributions accurately;
- respect confidentiality and other people's private information;
- avoid harassment, threats, discrimination, stalking, sexual harassment, intimidation or unwanted personal attention;
- avoid impersonation, deliberate deception or evasion of moderation actions;
- avoid using employer, academic, maintainer, financial or founder status to bypass technical review or community rules;
- respect scientific, licensing and data provenance even when doing so slows a desired feature;
- stop disruptive or boundary-crossing behavior when an authorized moderator asks for it to stop.

Good-faith technical criticism is welcome. Statements such as “this evidence does not support the claim,” “this API violates the authority contract,” or “this proposal should be rejected” are legitimate when focused on the work. The Code of Conduct must not be used to suppress falsification, dissent or evidence-based criticism.

## Scope

This policy applies to repository issues, pull requests, reviews, project-controlled discussions, project events and other official OFU community spaces. It also applies when a person is formally representing OFU in public.

Private behavior outside project spaces is not automatically project business. It becomes relevant when it materially threatens participant safety, constitutes targeted harassment connected to the project, or directly undermines the integrity of project-controlled spaces.

## Reporting a conduct issue

Sensitive conduct reports should be handled privately. **Do not publish personal, medical, identifying, harassment or other sensitive incident details in a public GitHub issue.**

While OFU remains at the `BOOTSTRAP` governance stage, the repository owner is the bootstrap moderation escalation point. A durable private community-reporting channel is a pre-public-scale administration task tracked in issue #266.

Until that channel exists, a participant who cannot reach a maintainer privately may open a minimal public issue titled `[moderation contact request]` with no incident details, solely to request a private contact path. Substantive reporting should move out of public view before sensitive information is shared.

A report may include, when safe and relevant:

- what happened and where;
- approximate time/date;
- people directly involved;
- links or preserved evidence;
- immediate safety concerns;
- whether the reporter wants confidentiality or attribution;
- any conflict of interest involving a moderator.

Knowingly false reports intended to harass another participant are themselves misconduct. An unsubstantiated good-faith report is not misconduct merely because evidence is incomplete.

## Moderation principles

Moderators should prioritize safety, proportionality, consistency, confidentiality and the minimum disclosure necessary to resolve the issue. Decisions should be based on available evidence and community impact rather than popularity, contributor rank or commercial importance.

A person who is directly involved in a report must not adjudicate that report when another qualified moderator is available. As the community grows, OFU should maintain more than one independent moderation contact to reduce conflicts of interest and bus factor.

## Enforcement ladder

Depending on severity and context, moderators may skip steps.

1. **Clarification or correction** — identify a boundary or rule and request a specific change in behavior.
2. **Formal warning** — privately document unacceptable behavior and consequences for recurrence.
3. **Time-limited restriction** — temporarily restrict reviews, comments, direct interaction, events or other community participation.
4. **Suspension** — remove participation privileges for a defined period and state conditions for return.
5. **Permanent removal** — remove access to project-controlled community spaces when severe or repeated conduct makes safe participation impracticable.

Content may also be edited, hidden, locked or removed when necessary to stop harm, protect private information, prevent spam/abuse or preserve a usable technical record.

## Appeals and review

A moderated participant may request one review of a significant restriction or ban when there is a materially different fact, procedural conflict, or credible reason the decision was disproportionate. Where possible, someone other than the original decision-maker should conduct the review.

An appeal is not an entitlement to repeated relitigation or renewed contact with people who asked for no contact.

## Transparency

OFU should document moderation rules publicly while keeping individual reports private by default. As governance matures, the project may publish aggregate moderation statistics or anonymized process improvements when doing so does not expose reporters or affected participants.

The current project-wide governance maturity state is recorded in `config/governance/community-resilience.json`; target-state moderation structures must not be described as operational before they are actually staffed.
